Effort is an inference control

An effort setting influences how much the model deliberates while producing a response. It is separate from architecture, stored parameters and training optimizer choice. It is not a fixed promise of a particular number of tokens or a guarantee that a hard problem will be solved.

Three controls, three questions

reasoning_effort asks how much reasoning effort to use. enable_thinking controls whether thinking is enabled. preserve_thinking controls retention of earlier thinking in the conversation format. Do not use low effort as a synonym for thinking disabled. Consult the serving engine’s supported request schema.

Optimize completed tasks

Per-response latency answers how soon one response arrives. Task latency also includes tool work, verification and retries. In a simplified independent-retry model with success probability p, the expected attempts are 1/p. If each attempt costs t seconds, expected time is t/p. Real retries may be correlated or capped; then this model no longer describes them accurately.

Build a small evaluation

Use representative easy and difficult tasks with checkable outcomes. Sweep supported effort settings while holding the model, tools, sampling configuration and output limits fixed. Record completion success, total tokens, time and retries. Choose a policy on held-out tasks. A stronger setting may pay for itself on complex jobs but waste resources on easy ones.

Worked example

Toy low effort costs 2 seconds with p = 0.25: expected time is 8 seconds. Toy xhigh costs 5 seconds with p = 1: time is 5 seconds. These are invented values showing how retries can reverse the ranking; they are not Qwen benchmark results.
